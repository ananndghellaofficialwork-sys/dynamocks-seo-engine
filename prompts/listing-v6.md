# Listing copy — v6

New file, not an edit to v5. This is the first version written against REAL
SEARCH DEMAND rather than against the product alone.

Everything before v6 answered *"what is this sock?"* — from the photograph,
the fibre spec, the tags. That question is now answered well. It was the wrong
question to stop at, because it never asked *"what does a shopper type?"*

Three independent sources were measured on 2026-08-21 and they agree:

| word type | Google impressions | on-site searches |
|---|---|---|
| **length** (ankle, short, crew) | **58%** | 15% |
| **colour** (green, purple, mango…) | **51%** | **35%** |
| pattern (polka, geometric, hexagon) | **2.5%** | 11% |

**v4 and v5 ordered the model to lead with the specific motif — hexagon, maze,
prism. Pattern words are 2.5% of demand.** That instruction is withdrawn here.

Autocomplete added the fourth finding: **"for men" / "for women" is the most
common modifier in this entire market**, and v3–v5 all forbade it.

---

## What actually gets searched — use these words

**Colour and length carry the traffic.** Lead with them.

**Gender is the biggest modifier there is.** `men's dress socks`,
`over the calf socks for men`, `cotton crew socks for men`, `funky socks for
women`, `bamboo socks for men`. Earlier versions banned this to stop invented
facts. The ban is lifted with one condition below.

**Say "gray" as well as "grey".** Eleven on-site searches for `gray` find
nothing, because every listing in this catalog spells it the British way. The
audience is American.

**"Cool" outranks "colorful".** Autocomplete replaces `colorful socks for men`
with `cool socks for men`. Prefer *cool*, *fun*, *funky*, *bold* over
*colorful* and *vibrant*.

**"Short" is a real synonym for ankle** — 1,471 impressions, and not one
existing title uses it. Where it fits naturally, use it.

**Words that are searched and never appear in this catalog:** `argyle`,
`solid`, `houndstooth`. If the photograph shows argyle, say argyle.

**Filler that costs characters and earns nothing.** Never write: *Single Pair*,
*Fruity Fun*, *Cotton Comfort*, *Shop for*, *Premium Quality*, *Ultimate*,
*Experience the*, *Elevate your*. One current live title reads
`Mango Crew Socks | Single Pair, Fruity Fun, Cotton Comfort` — it ranks at
position 8.7 for `mango socks`, 739 impressions, **one click**. The filler is
why.

---

## Grounding — unchanged, and it still outranks everything

1. **Look at the photographs first.** They decide what the product IS.
2. **The text fields cross-check.** They confirm what a photo cannot show.
3. **Internal names are never grounding.** `Polka`, `Rogue`, `TANGY`, `MONO`,
   `TRIOS`, `Stripes 21.0` are merchandising labels. Describe what you see.
4. **If image and text disagree, the photo wins silently.** Refuse ONLY on
   colour, pack count, material or length — the four claims that would make a
   customer feel misled when the parcel arrives.
5. **Ignore banners and size charts** among the photographs.

### The rule that outranks the keyword list

**Grounding beats search volume, always.**

If a phrase has 700 impressions and this product does not match it, do not use
it. `mango socks` is worth 739 impressions — it goes on a mango sock and
nowhere else. A high-volume lie is still a lie, and it arrives at a real
customer's door.

---

## FIRST — which kind of product is this?

The message states **PRODUCT KIND** as `single`, `multipack` or `dress`.
Decided in code from the listing data. Do not second-guess it.

They sell to three different searches. Follow the matching path.

---

## PATH A — single pair, casual

**Shape:** `<Colour> <Motif> <Length> Socks for Men & Women`

Colour first. It is half the demand and 44% of current proposed titles have no
colour word at all.

| Weak | Better |
|---|---|
| Geometric Print Crew Socks | Black & White Hexagon Crew Socks |
| Colorful Striped Crew Socks | Red & Navy Striped Crew Socks |
| Houndstooth Crew Socks | Black & White Houndstooth Crew Socks |
| Leopard Print Crew Socks | Tan & Black Leopard Print Crew Socks |

- Under 60 characters. Google truncates near there.
- Motif is SECONDARY — include it only if the colour and length fit first.
- Use the plain word: *polka dot* not *pin-dot*, *geometric* not *tessellated*.
- Never stack two words for one idea. "Geometric Block Pattern" is one idea.
- Drop `| Dynamocks` before you drop the colour. The brand is a bonus.

## PATH B — multipack, combo, gift box

**Shape:** `<N>-Pair <Family> Sock Gift Set for Men`

Autocomplete confirms the exact terms: `sock gift box men`, `sock gift set
men`, `mens dress sock gift box`. Nobody shopping a gift box searches for a
pattern name.

- **Pack count leads.** It is the fact the shopper is checking for.
- **Never name the individual designs in the title.** `Bubbles`, `Pizzazz`,
  `Maze` have no search volume and would spend half the character budget.
  They belong in the description, where the reader is already on the page.
- Use *Gift Set* or *Gift Box* only when the listing supports it.
- Family word generically: striped, patterned, solid, novelty, designer.

| Weak | Better |
|---|---|
| Cotton Crew Socks Combo: Set of 4 Gift Box | 4-Pair Designer Sock Gift Box for Men |
| Stripes DUO 2.0, X-3, X-4 Combo | 4-Pair Striped Crew Sock Set for Men |

## PATH C — dress and formal

**This is the revenue line and the segment the store is most invisible for.**
Top sellers are men's striped dress-sock bundles. Search Console shows 58
impressions for "dress" in three months — that is not low demand, it is low
visibility. Boardroom Socks, Southern Scholar, The Tie Bar and Aklasu are
entire businesses built on this term.

**Shape:** `<Length> <Material> Dress Socks for Men`

**Material is REQUIRED here and omitted everywhere else.** Competitor titles
lead with it — *Mercerized Cotton*, *Egyptian Cotton*, *Cotton Lisle*, *Merino
Wool*. In the casual segment material is 8% of demand; in dress it is a
headline feature. Take it from the `material composition` field only.

- Say **Dress Socks**, not "Crew Socks", when the product is a dress sock.
- **"for Men"** — this segment searches by gender more than any other.
- Colour and pattern come after: `vertical striped dress socks`,
  `blue striped dress socks` are real queries.
- Where the body copy supports it, **"stay up"** is worth more than any
  adjective. `mens dress socks that stay up` and `over the calf socks that
  stay up` both autocomplete — it is a pain point, and this catalog's own copy
  already promises "a secure, stay-put fit".

| Weak | Better |
|---|---|
| Dynamocks Executive Striped Cotton Dress Crew Socks – Stripes 21.0 | Striped Combed Cotton Dress Socks for Men |
| Cotton Designer Stripes Crew Length Dress Socks Combo Shop for Men & Women | 6-Pair Striped Cotton Dress Sock Gift Box for Men |

### Bamboo is a special case within dress

The demand is **health and comfort**, not sustainability: `bamboo socks
benefits`, `bamboo socks for neuropathy`, `bamboo socks reviews`. Lead with
softness, breathability and odour control. Do not lead with eco-friendly.

---

## Do not invent facts

- **Material:** from the `material composition` field and nothing else. If it
  reads `(NOT STATED …)`, name no material at all — not "cotton", not
  "premium fabric". The Artistic Expression Pack is 75% polyester and its own
  marketing copy calls it "cotton-rich". That is the error this rule exists
  for.
- **Pack count:** from the text only, never counted off a photograph. Six
  socks in a picture may be three pairs, or one pair from six angles.
- **Gender — the ban is lifted, with one condition.** "for Men & Women" is
  TRUE for this catalog: it is the stated default in the store's own body
  copy, and it is the most-searched modifier in the market. Use it freely.
  Narrow to "for Men" ALONE only where the listing genuinely is a men's
  product — the dress and executive lines say so in their own titles. Never
  narrow to "for Women" without the same evidence.
- **Length:** crew, ankle, no-show, quarter — from the text, never guessed.

## The page title — product_title

A THIRD field, and it is treated differently from the other two.

`product_title` is the store's **H1** — the heading on the page, the text in
every collection grid, the line in the cart, and what a social share displays.
It is the strongest on-page ranking signal there is, and Google rewrites title
tags roughly 60% of the time, reaching for the H1 when it does. A weak H1 can
override a good meta title.

**It is NEVER pushed automatically.** `push.py` refuses it by name. What you
write here becomes a suggestion in a review file, and the store owner applies
it by hand if she agrees. Write it as a recommendation to a person, not as a
command to a machine.

What is wrong with the current ones, measured across 366 live titles:

- **183 open with "Dynamocks."** The brand is the one word a stranger is not
  searching for. It belongs at the end or nowhere.
- **191 end in a trailing codename** — `– Crew`, `– Banger`, `– Stripes 21.0`.
  "– Crew" on a title that already says "Crew Socks" is pure waste.
- **102 exceed 60 characters.**
- **Only 45% name a colour**, and colour is half of all demand.

**Shape:** `<Colour> <Motif> <Length> Socks` — the same priority as seo_title,
because the same shopper reads both.

| Current | Better |
|---|---|
| Dynamocks Allure Colorful Designer Cotton Crew Socks – Crew | Red & Teal Scallop Print Crew Socks |
| Dynamocks Bold Patterned Cotton Crew Socks – Banger | Blue, Red & Yellow Zigzag Crew Socks |
| Dynamocks Executive Striped Cotton Dress Crew Socks – Stripes 21.0 | Striped Cotton Dress Socks for Men |

**It may differ from seo_title, and usually should.** seo_title carries
`| Dynamocks` for the search result; the H1 does not need to — the shopper is
already on your site and can see whose store it is. Keep them thematically
identical and never contradictory: the same colour, the same motif, the same
length. A page whose H1 says hexagon and whose meta title says geometric is
the drift the append-only proposals table exists to catch.

**Keep it human.** This is a heading a customer reads, not a keyword slot. It
must still sound like something a person would write on a shelf label.

## Output format

Four labelled lines, nothing else — no preamble, no markdown fences:

```
grounding: <what you saw, what you rejected, which search term you chose and why>
seo_title: <the meta title — the blue link in Google>
seo_description: <the meta description>
product_title: <the page heading — a suggestion, never auto-applied>
```

`grounding` first, so the reasoning shapes the answer instead of excusing it.
One or two sentences covering:

1. **What the photographs show** — the colours and motif, and which frames you
   ignored as banners or size charts.
2. **Which path and why** — single, multipack or dress.
3. **Which searched phrase you aimed at**, and what you rejected. Name the
   word. "Chose 'green ankle' over 'geometric' — colour and length carry the
   demand and geometric is 2.5% of it."

Useless — do not do this:

> grounding: The images show the product clearly and the title reflects it.

Good:

> grounding: Frames 2-4 show black and grey interlocking hexagons; frame 1 is a
> sale banner. Led with "Black & White Hexagon" over "Geometric Print" — colour
> is half of all demand and the generic geometric title is already taken by
> four other products.

If it cannot be grounded, return the grounding line and then:

```
needs_human: <one line naming what could not be resolved>
```

---

## The description

- **Lead with what makes THIS product specific**, not with generic sock
  attributes.
- **Work in one real search phrase** where it fits truthfully — the message
  lists the phrases that actually get typed.
- **Vary the sentence shape between products.** If the description reads as
  the previous one with the noun swapped, rewrite it. That uniformity is the
  defect v2 was written to kill and it is still the easiest failure to fall
  into.
- Say who it suits and when: work, gifting, everyday, travel.
- Material and fit are optional, not mandatory filler — except on Path C,
  where material earns its place.
- Both fields, every time. Never one without the other.
